Modern Axiomatics, Synthetic Mastery & Geometric Noetics¶

David Hilbert · Bertrand Russell · Nathan Altshiller-Court · Petrus Hoenen · Fred W. Seelig

David Hilbert (1862–1943)¶

  • GEOMETOR Edition: hilbert — Grundlagen der Geometrie (Repo)

In Grundlagen der Geometrie (1899), Hilbert completed Euclid’s deductive program for the modern era by making explicit the order (betweenness) and continuity postulates tacitly read off diagrams since antiquity, constructing an exact segment calculus directly from Desargues’s and Pascal’s incidence theorems, and proving the mutual independence of axiom groups via algebraic number-field models.

Bertrand Russell (1872–1970)¶

  • GEOMETOR Edition: bertrand-russell — Space & Foundations of Geometry (Repo)

Across An Essay on the Foundations of Geometry (1897) and Part VI (Space) of The Principles of Mathematics (1903), Bertrand Russell analyzed the philosophical relationship between projective order, Cayley–Klein metrical geometry, non-Euclidean spaces of constant curvature, and relational logic—providing a decisive foil for twentieth-century debates over intuition and formalism.

Nathan Altshiller-Court (1881–1968)¶

  • GEOMETOR Edition: altshiller-court — College Geometry (Repo)

Master pedagogue of modern synthetic geometry whose College Geometry (1925; expanded 2nd ed., 1952) systematically trains the geometer in the four-part constructive discipline (Analysis, Construction, Proof, Discussion), connecting Euclid directly to the Nine-Point Circle, Feuerbach’s tangency theorem, Simson lines, harmonic bundles, inversion, and Lemoine–Brocard triangle geometry.

Petrus Hoenen, S.J. (1880–1961)¶

  • GEOMETOR Edition: hoenen — De Noetica Geometriae (1954) (Repo)

Dutch Jesuit physicist and philosopher at the Pontifical Gregorian University in Rome. In De Noetica Geometriae Origine Theoriae Cognitionis (1954), Hoenen engaged Hilbert, Russell, and non-Euclidean geometry directly—showing that axiomatization presupposes a prior noetic act of intuitive formal abstraction, in which the intellect perceives necessary structural nexus within the concrete spatial continuum. Hoenen’s epistemology anchors GEOMETOR’s principle of earned understanding.

Fred W. Seelig¶

  • GEOMETOR Edition: fundamentals — The Fred W. Seelig Archive (Repo)

Independent geometric researcher whose meticulous 353-folio manuscript archive and 59 analytical spreadsheets explore triangle concurrency, harmonic duality, circle–square proportions, and classical ratios from first principles—reconstructed into executable symbolic models across 55 topics (F.1–F.55) in geometor/fundamentals.
