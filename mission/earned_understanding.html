Earned Understanding & Geometric Noetics¶

Projection, Selection, and Intuitive Formal Abstraction

How does the human intellect—or a reasoning machine—arrive at universal, necessary geometric truth from a single constructed diagram?

This epistemological question stands at the heart of GEOMETOR’s Historical & Axiomatic Scriptorium, tracing a continuous 2,300-year conversation from antiquity to twentieth-century foundations:

  1. Aristotle (aristotle): In Posterior Analytics and Metaphysics, scientific knowledge (episteme) requires grasping the reasoned fact (to dioti)—seeing why the angles of a triangle equal two right angles the instant the parallel or exterior construction is drawn (Metaph. IX.9, 1051a21–33).

  2. Proclus (proclus): In his Commentary on Book I of Euclid’s Elements, the geometric imagination (phantasia) serves as an active screen onto which the understanding (dianoia) projects (probole) pure unextended ratios so that the intellect can inspect their divisions and symmetries.

  3. Thomas Aquinas (aquinas): In Super Boetium De Trinitate (qq. 5–6), mathematical science abstracts from sensible matter while retaining intelligible matter (materia intelligibilis)—the pure continuum of extension in which distinct circles and triangles can be instantiated, compared, and intersected, always terminating (judicium) in imaginative construction.

  4. Hilbert & Bertrand Russell (hilbert, bertrand-russell): At the turn of the twentieth century, Hilbert’s Grundlagen der Geometrie (1899) and Russell’s Foundations of Geometry (1897) stripped away unstated diagrammatic assumptions, isolating incidence, order, congruence, parallel, and continuity axioms and testing their logical independence through explicit algebraic models.

  5. Petrus Hoenen, S.J. (hoenen): In De Noetica Geometriae (1954), Hoenen answered pure formalism by recovering intuitive formal abstraction: axioms are not arbitrary rules of a symbol game, because the necessity of Euclidean nexus is first apprehended directly within concrete spatial construction before being codified into formal postulates.

Projection and Selection in geometor.model¶

GEOMETOR translates this classical epistemology directly into software architecture:

  • Projection (*Probole*): Constructing [ A B ] or ( A B ) projects a continuous geometric law across the entire plane.

  • Selection (*Judicium*): Intersecting two projections E₁ ∩ E₂ selects exact algebraic points whose coordinates and ancestral dependency DAG record every postulate and prior locus required to bring them into being.

  • Earned Understanding in AI (`seer`): Rather than training opaque neural networks to guess pixel grids directly, geometor/seer pairs hypothesis imagination (Dreamer) with verifiable symbolic Python synthesis (Coder)—requiring every proposed insight to earn its validity against exact structural constraints.
