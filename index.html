GEOMETOR¶

Exploring the Architecture of All That Is

GEOMETOR is the central portal and gateway to the interconnected semantic digital archive, symbolic software engine, and digital scriptorium investigating geometry as humanity’s most concise language for documenting nature, proportion, and deductive truth.

When we watch a botanical meristem pack florets at the golden angle, examine crystal lattices, or trace projective collineations, one foundational question recurs: How do the simplest axiomatic relationships unfold into the full complexity of form and mind?

Across sixty-two interconnected repositories, GEOMETOR recasts 2,400 years of classical treatises, projective and rational geometry, natural morphogenesis, epistemological philosophy, and machine reasoning into one consistent, exact symbolic grammar—uniting two complementary modes of work:

  • Scholarly Editions (*72 Book Codices · 39 Authors*): Multi-language critical editions of public-domain works (Greek, Latin, Italian, German, French, and English) paired with over 8,900 tracked primary-source folio scans and calibrated .euclid-graphic SVGs.

  • Mathematical Catalogs (*12 Compendia*): One-item-per-page symbolic catalogs documenting the dependency graphs, golden constructions, triangle centers, triangle cubics, polyhedra, rational trigonometry laws, Fibonacci identities, and proving engines of modern mathematics (elements, etc, gibert, wildberger, fundamentals, divine, polyhedra, knott, huntley, livio, posamentier, codes) with full scholarly citation.

From Two Points in an Empty Field¶

Every GEOMETOR construction begins on an unmarked planar field with the minimum possible information: two given points, A and B. Their separation establishes the unit measure (\(1\)). Rather than approximating coordinates in floating-point arithmetic, we project continuous straight lines and circles from known points and select exact algebraic intersections.

Use the playback controls below the figure (or hover any highlighted symbol in the steps) to watch how two points unfold through the Vesica Piscis and Equidistant Poles into the Divine Proportion:

given A, B — Two starting points at \((0, 0)\) and \((1, 0)\) establish the planar field, unit segment / A B /, and infinite baseline [ A B ].
construct circle(A, B) → ( A B ), circle(B, A) → ( B A ) — Sweeping two symmetric unit circles forms the Vesica Piscis.
intersect ( A B ) ∩ ( B A ) → C, C′ — Their intersections select the Equidistant Poles, unfolding the equilateral triangle < A B C > (equilateral_triangle), rational spread \(s = \frac{3}{4}\) (\(\sqrt{3}\)), and the orthogonal axis [ C C′ ] bisecting / A B / at midpoint M.
construct Extending rays [ C′ P ] and [ C′ Q ] through A and B to the twin circles forms the enlarged side-\(2\) equilateral frame < C′ P Q >; its medians [ A Q ] and [ B P ] meet [ C C′ ] at circumcenter O, from which we sweep the outer circumcircle ( O C′ ).
∴

( O C′ ) ∩ [ A B ] → H, G — By George Odom’s midline theorem, / A B / and / B G / divide / A G / in Extreme and Mean Ratio:

\[\frac{|AG|}{|AB|} = \frac{|AB|}{|BG|} = \phi = \frac{1 + \sqrt{5}}{2}\]

From this single unfolding emerge the regular pentagon and icosahedron, projective harmonic ranges, rational spread polynomials, Fibonacci parastichies in living plants, and formal constructive proofs.

One Language of Form: Method & Principles¶

  • Exact Symbolic Algebra over Floating-Point Approximation: Dynamic geometry tools built on IEEE-754 floats can only report that two segments look numerically close to \(1.6180339\dots\) Powered by SymPy, geometor.model preserves every coordinate, polynomial root, quadrance, spread, and cross-ratio in exact algebraic form—proving mathematical identity rather than decimal coincidence.

  • Projection & Selection: Points hold information; lines [ A B ] and circles ( A B ) project infinite loci across the field; intersections ∩ select derived points carrying their complete ancestral dependency DAG back to the initial givens.

  • Earned Understanding (*Noetics of Geometry*): Grounded in Plato, Aristotle, Proclus, Thomas Aquinas (materia intelligibilis), Nicholas of Cusa, and Petrus Hoenen’s De Noetica Geometriae, geometric necessity is neither empirical guesswork nor an uninterpreted game of marks—it is apprehended directly within concrete construction.

  • Scholarly Editions & Mathematical Catalogs: Powered by photon-platform/publish, GEOMETOR pairs multi-track Book Codices of historical public-domain sources with structured one-item-per-page Mathematical Catalogs for modern research works.

I. The Symbolic Core, Discovery & Archive Architecture¶

  • codes · elementarium · press · animations · trees: Theorem-proving engine Catalog, 100,000-year optical bootstrap archive, Digital Scriptorium Press shelf, proportional studies, and monotone planar tree layouts.

II. The Unfolding Canon (The Elementrium & Constructive Geometry)¶

  • euclid · elements · byrne: Heath’s 13-Book Elements (1,416 folios, 831 calibrated SVGs) & Data, the 601-node G-Index (elements2/), and Oliver Byrne’s 1847 chromatic Elements (339 RGB folios).

  • archimedes · apollonius · heron · menelaus · ptolemy · pappus: Seven treatises of Archimedes, Apollonius’s Conics & reconstructed works, four treatises of Heron (Metrica, Definitiones, Catoptrica, Dioptra), Menelaus’s Sphaerica, Ptolemy’s Almagest & Planisphaerium (1,054 folios), and Pappus’s Synagoge.

  • pascal · ceva · euler · mascheroni · feuerbach · steiner: Pascal’s 1640 Essay pour les Coniques, Ceva’s 1678 statical concurrency, Euler’s 1765 Solutio facilis (E325), Mascheroni’s 1797 compass-only geometry, Feuerbach’s 1822 Nine-Point tangency, and Steiner’s 1833 fixed-circle straightedge constructions.

  • staudt · petersen · casey · klein · lemoine · altshiller-court: von Staudt’s Geometrie der Lage & Beiträge, Petersen’s Methods and Theories, Casey’s Sequel to Euclid, Klein’s Erlangen Program & Famous Problems, Lemoine’s Géométrographie, and Altshiller-Court’s College Geometry.

  • etc · gibert · wildberger · fundamentals: Catalogs of Kimberling’s Encyclopedia of Triangle Centers (4,000+ compiled centers, 16,000+ SVGs), Gibert’s Catalogue of Triangle Cubics, Wildberger’s Divine Proportions & Rational Trigonometry, and the 353-folio Fred W. Seelig Archive.

III. Philosophical, Renaissance, Harmonic & Axiomatic Scriptorium¶

  • fibonacci · aquinas · cusa: Fibonacci’s Liber Abbaci & Practica Geometriae (762 folios), six Thomistic treatises on materia intelligibilis, and Nicholas of Cusa’s De Docta Ignorantia & De Mathematicis Complementis.

  • piero · pacioli · durer · kepler: Piero della Francesca’s Libellus de Quinque Corporibus Regularibus, Pacioli’s De Divina Proportione (Leonardo’s 60 woodcuts) & Summa (1494), Dürer’s Underweysung der Messung (1525), and Kepler’s Mysterium, Strena, & Harmonices Mundi (552 folios & interactive 3D nested Platonic solids).

  • pasch · hilbert · poincare · bertrand-russell · hoenen · russell: Pasch’s Vorlesungen (1882), Hilbert’s Grundlagen (1899), Poincaré’s Science and Hypothesis, Bertrand Russell’s Foundations & Principles, Hoenen’s De Noetica Geometriae (1954), and Walter & Lao Russell’s Home Study Course (519 folios) & Wave-Field Cosmogony.
