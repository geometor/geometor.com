Classical Antiquity: Demonstration, Elements, Conics & Projection
=================================================================

.. rubric:: Plato · Aristotle · Euclid · Archimedes · Apollonius · Heron · Menelaus · Nicomachus · Ptolemy · Pappus · Proclus

----

Plato (c. 427–347 BC)
---------------------

* **GEOMETOR Edition**: `plato — Geometric & Mathematical Dialogues <https://geometor.github.io/plato/>`_ (`Repo <https://github.com/geometor/plato>`_)

Across *Meno* (doubling the square via the diagonal), *Republic* VI–VII (the **Divided Line** and the four mathematical sciences lifting the soul toward Being), and *Timaeus* (the harmonic :math:`2^a 3^b` World-Soul scale and construction of the regular cosmic polyhedra from half-square and half-equilateral right triangles), Plato established mathematical proportion as the intelligible bond (*desmos*) uniting multiplicity into cosmos.

----

Aristotle (384–322 BC)
----------------------

* **GEOMETOR Edition**: `aristotle — Loci Geometrici et Noetici <https://geometor.github.io/aristotle/>`_ (`Repo <https://github.com/geometor/aristotle>`_)

In *Posterior Analytics*, *Physics*, *De Anima*, and *Metaphysics* IX.9 (1051a21–33), Aristotle formulated the classical theory of **demonstrative science** (*episteme*) and observed how latent geometric relations are brought from potency into luminous actuality **by the act of dividing and constructing the figure**: *"If the lines had already been divided, the theorems would have been obvious as soon as seen."*

----

Euclid of Alexandria (fl. c. 300 BC)
------------------------------------

* **GEOMETOR Editions**: `euclid <https://geometor.github.io/euclid/>`_ (Heath 13-Book Critical Codex + *Data* & *Optics*) · `elements <https://geometor.github.io/elements/>`_ (601-Node G-Index) · `YouTube Playlist <https://www.youtube.com/playlist?list=PLUXlBs_QySgw>`_

Euclid's thirteen books of the *Elements*—paired in ``geometor/euclid`` with his **94-proposition *Data*** (governing constructive *givens* in geometric analysis) and **58-proposition *Optics*** (visual cones and angular perspective)—established the enduring paradigm of deductive architecture, culminating in **Extreme and Mean Ratio** (*II.11*, *VI.30*) and the **five Platonic solids** (*Book XIII*).

----

Archimedes of Syracuse (c. 287–212 BC)
--------------------------------------

* **GEOMETOR Edition**: `archimedes — Opera Geometrica <https://geometor.github.io/archimedes/>`_ (`Repo <https://github.com/geometor/archimedes>`_)

Greatest mathematician of antiquity. In *Measurement of a Circle* (:math:`3\frac{10}{71} < \pi < 3\frac{1}{7}`), *Quadrature of the Parabola* (exact :math:`\frac{4}{3}` triangle ratio), *On the Sphere and Cylinder* (:math:`2:3` volume and surface ratio), *On Spirals*, *Book of Lemmas* (*arbelos*, *salinon*, trisection), and *The Method of Mechanical Theorems*, Archimedes united mechanical lever equilibrium with rigorous exhaustion proofs.

----

Apollonius of Perga (c. 240 – c. 190 BC)
----------------------------------------

* **GEOMETOR Edition**: `apollonius — Conics & Lost Tangency Works <https://geometor.github.io/apollonius/>`_ (`Repo <https://github.com/geometor/apollonius>`_)

"The Great Geometer," whose *Conics* derived the **parabola**, **ellipse**, and **hyperbola** from a single oblique circular cone through symptomata of **application of areas** (*parabole*, *elleipsis*, *hyperbole*), conjugate diameters, polar tangents, and harmonic division—alongside the famous **Problem of Apollonius** (*On Tangencies*, ten tangent circles to three given circles).

----

Heron of Alexandria (c. 10–70 AD)
---------------------------------

* **GEOMETOR Edition**: `heron — Metrica & Catoptrica <https://geometor.github.io/heron/>`_ (`Repo <https://github.com/geometor/heron>`_)

Synthesist of Alexandrian pure proof and algorithmic engineering. In *Metrica* (rediscovered in 1896 in the *Codex Constantinopolitanus*), Heron proved **Heron's Area Formula** :math:`\Delta = \sqrt{s(s-a)(s-b)(s-c)}` via cyclic quadrilaterals alongside iterative rational root extraction; in *Catoptrica*, he gave the first **minimum-path reflection proof** of equal incidence and reflection angles.

----

Menelaus of Alexandria (c. 70–140 AD)
-------------------------------------

* **GEOMETOR Edition**: `menelaus — Sphaerica <https://geometor.github.io/menelaus/>`_ (`Repo <https://github.com/geometor/menelaus>`_)

Founder of intrinsic spherical geometry. Defining the **spherical triangle** formed by arcs of three great circles (*Sphaerica* Book I, where angle sums strictly exceed two right angles without parallel lines) and proving the **Menelaus Transversal Theorem** (Book III.1, collinearity of three side-points via signed ratio product :math:`-1` and six-quantity spherical chord ratios), Menelaus provided the engine of both celestial trigonometry and projective duality.

----

Nicomachus of Gerasa (c. 60–120 AD)
-----------------------------------

* **GEOMETOR Edition**: `nicomachus — Introduction to Arithmetic <https://geometor.github.io/nicomachus/>`_ (`Repo <https://github.com/geometor/nicomachus>`_)

Neo-Pythagorean philosopher whose *Introductio Arithmetica* codified the fourfold **Quadrivium** (*Arithmetic, Music, Geometry, Astronomy*), proved that all superparticular and superpartient ratios unfold deductively from pure **Equality** :math:`(1, 1, 1)` via his three-term rule, classified polygonal gnomons and the ten classical means, and celebrated the cube's :math:`6:8::9:12` harmonic proportion.

----

Claudius Ptolemy (c. 100–170 AD)
--------------------------------

* **GEOMETOR Edition**: `ptolemy — Almagest Book I & Planisphaerium <https://geometor.github.io/ptolemy/>`_ (`Repo <https://github.com/geometor/ptolemy>`_)

In *Almagest* Book I.10, Ptolemy gave antiquity's most economical **single-circle construction of both the regular decagon and regular pentagon** via the Golden Section alongside **Ptolemy's Cyclic Quadrilateral Theorem** (:math:`AC \cdot BD = AB \cdot CD + BC \cdot DA`) to compute exact sexagesimal chord tables; in *Planisphaerium*, he proved the circle-preserving property of **stereographic projection** underlying the astrolabe and conformal geometry.

----

Pappus of Alexandria (c. 290 – c. 350 AD)
-----------------------------------------

* **GEOMETOR Edition**: `pappus — Synagoge & Projective Geometry <https://geometor.github.io/pappus/>`_ (`Repo <https://github.com/geometor/pappus>`_)

Last great synthesist of the Alexandrian mathematical school. Across Books III–VIII of his *Synagoge* (*Mathematical Collection*), Pappus united the three classical means in one semicircle (Book III), generalized Pythagoras's theorem and proved the infinite *arbelos* circle chain :math:`y_n = n d_n` (Book IV), proved the **isoperimetric optimality of the bee's hexagonal honeycomb** (Book V), preserved the **Treasury of Analysis**, **Pappus–Guldin centroid rule**, **cross-ratio invariance**, and **Pappus's Hexagon Theorem** (Book VII), and systematized **architectonic mechanics** (Book VIII).

----

Proclus Lycius (412–485 AD)
---------------------------

* **GEOMETOR Edition**: `proclus — Commentary on Euclid Book I <https://geometor.github.io/proclus/>`_ (`Repo <https://github.com/geometor/proclus>`_)

Head (*diadochus*) of the Athenian Academy whose *Commentary on the First Book of Euclid's Elements* preserves both Eudemus's lost history of early Greek geometry and a profound philosophy of **mathematical imagination** (*phantasia*). For Proclus, geometric construction is **projection** (*probole*): the discursive intellect projects indivisible ratios onto the extended screen of imagination so that universal form can be contemplated in concrete multiplicity.
